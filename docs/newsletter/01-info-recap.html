Welcome to TRSC!

Come explore science with us.

Hi there,

Welcome to the Turtle Rock Science Club Lab Log, our weekly newsletter! Thank you so much for joining us.

TRSC began in 2023 as a small group of students meeting at park picnic tables to explore science together. Since then, we have grown into an active community supporting students across Turtle Rock Elementary, University Park Elementary, and Bonita Canyon Elementary. Our coaches are high school and middle school coaches from University High School, Rancho San Joaquin Middle School, and Sage Hill — and our team proudly qualified for the Division B State Tournament!

Our 4 Core Goals
01. Foster a love of science: Inspire lasting curiosity beyond rote memorization.
02. Build community: Connect students and families who encourage and support each other.
03. Build knowledge: Hands-on experiments, Science Olympiad training, and guest seminars.
04. Build leadership skills: Learn and grow through teaching, collaboration, and leading.

Science tidbits
Chemistry
Adding salt shrinks water
If you add salt to a full glass of water, it won't overflow - the water level will actually go down. This is because salt absorbs some of the water.
Astronomy
Planets rain diamonds!
On icy giants like Neptune and Uranus, the atmosphere has a lot of pressure, which squeezes carbon atoms together and forms actual diamonds that fall down as rain!
In the news
Artificial Intelligence
OpenAI Solves Navier-Stokes
The Navier-Stokes equations describe how fluids like air and water move. However, figuring out whether or not these equations fail at some point is one of the most difficult problems in all of physics and mathematics. In fact, whoever can figure out the answer gets $1 million from the Clay Institute! Recently, OpenAI, the company that makes ChatGPT, used a super-powerful version of ChatGPT that solved this problem, proving that the equations do break down under certain conditions.

Health & Medicine
Long-term health issues from the September 11th Attacks
Marking the 25th anniversary of September 11, ongoing studies show that thousands of first responders, recovery workers, and survivors that were on the ground during the terrorist attacks continue to battle severe long-term health problems, caused by the debris in the air, PTSD, and trauma.

Scientists Improve Quantum Computer Speeds by Up to 1,000x
Researchers at Chalmers University of Technology have figured out a way to make quantum computers up to 1,000x faster, while making the computers are less likely to make errors and break down. This is huge progress towards building a real-life quantum computer that can actually do useful stuff.

Science Olympiad & Tryouts
What is Science Olympiad?

Science Olympiad is a national team competition featuring 23 events spanning biology, chemistry, physics, earth science, and engineering. Teams consist of up to 15 students who collaborate in pairs to test their knowledge, lab techniques, and build devices.

Our students compete in Division B (Middle School level, with elementary students stepping up to compete). In prior seasons, our team has proudly qualified for the Division B State Tournament!

The 2026–2027 23 Competition Events
Life & Social Science: Anatomy & Physiology, Disease Detectives, Botany, Heredity, Water Quality
Earth & Space Science: Dynamic Planet, Meteorology, Remote Sensing, Rocks & Minerals, Solar System
Physical Science & Chem: Circuit Lab, Hovercraft, Crime Buster, Thermodynamics, Food Science
Technology & Engineering: Boomilever, Elastic Launched Gliders, Roller Coaster, Scrambler
Inquiry & Nature of Science: Codebusters, Experimental Design, Ping Pong Parachute, Write It Do It
Team Tryouts: October 24th & 25th

• Event Assignment: Students complete preference surveys; coaches match events based on student interests, strengths, and buddy requests.

Parent Involvement & Support
How parents can help

Turtle Rock Science Club thrives because of parent volunteers. Here is how you can get involved:

• Support student study: Check on progress, help with event coaching, and coordinate partner study sessions.
• Tournament days: Chaperone students, assist with logistics, coordinate food & snacks, and take photos.
• Guest speakers: Share your career in science, engineering, medicine, or tech during a seminar.
• Suggested donation ($30/student/year): Helps purchase workshop chemicals, lab kits, build materials, and experimental supplies.
How to sign up for an event
It takes about fifteen seconds and no account is required.
1. Go to trscienceclub.org and open Events.
2. Pick a session and hit Sign Up.
3. Enter the student's name and school. That's the whole form.
Want the full experience? You can also create a member profile to earn XP, collect badges, and save your details for 1-click sign-ups (see below).

How to create an account
Joining the club gives students their own profile, tracks lab minigame achievements and XP, and enables faster one-click sign-ups for future sessions.
1. Go to trscienceclub.org and click Join in the top header.
2. Enter your student's details (name, grade, school) and parent/guardian contact info.
3. Review and accept the short video consent and liability waivers.
4. Set a password (minimum 8 characters) to finish creating your account.
