💡 Science Tidbits: This Week’s Topics

Fascinating scientific principles previewing this week's workshops:

Environmental Science
Trees Have an Underground Internet: The “Wood Wide Web”!

Beneath forest soil lies a network of fungal threads connecting tree roots. Through this underground system, mature trees share nutrients with shaded saplings and send chemical distress warnings to alert neighbors before pests arrive!

Computer Science
The First Computer “Bug” Was a Real Moth!

In 1947, Grace Hopper's team opened the Harvard Mark II computer to fix a glitch and found a live moth trapped in Relay #70. They taped it into their logbook as the “first actual case of bug being found”—giving rise to “debugging”!

Physics
You Weigh Less at the Equator Than at the North Pole!

Because Earth spins at over 1,000 mph, inertia counters gravity slightly outward while the planet's equator bulges. Standing on a scale at the equator, you weigh about 0.5% less than at the North Pole!

First New Penguin Species Identified in Over 100 Years

Genetic analysis of gentoo penguins on the sub-Antarctic Kerguelen Islands revealed a distinct, unrecognized species—the first new penguin classification in over a century.

Big Bang “String Breaking” Recreated in Quantum Lab
