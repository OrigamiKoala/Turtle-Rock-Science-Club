💡 Science Tidbits: This Week’s Topics

Curious questions and real-world phenomena previewing this week's workshops:

Computer Science
The Internet is Guarded by 100 Lava Lamps!

Believe it or not, a huge chunk of the internet is protected by a wall of 100 colorful, bubbling lava lamps! Computers are so good at following mathematical rules that they struggle to invent truly random numbers. To generate unhackable security keys, cybersecurity engineers point a camera at glowing lava lamps—because the floating wax blobs morph unpredictably, their chaotic motion is turned into secret cryptographic keys for millions of websites!

Chemistry
Molecules Can Be “Left-Handed” or “Right-Handed”!

Hold up your hands—they look identical, but they are mirror twins: you can never stack them facing the exact same direction! In chemistry, this property is called chirality. Even when two molecules contain the exact same atoms in the same order, their mirror shapes can behave completely differently: for example, the “right-handed” version of limonene smells like juicy oranges, while the “left-handed” twin smells like pine trees!

Space & The Moon
NASA Spots a Giant Brand-New Crater on the Moon!

NASA’s Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter recently photographed a fresh 728-foot-wide impact crater on the Moon's far side! A space rock collided at hyper-velocity, blasting out a giant crater large enough to fit more than two full football fields.

Astronomy & Exoplanets
Astronomers Find the Youngest “Baby Planet” Ever Seen!

Astronomers have confirmed a Jupiter-sized gas giant that is less than one million years old. While that sounds ancient, in cosmic time it is a newborn infant world still actively accreting gas from a swirling primordial circumstellar disc!

NASA’s Next Giant Space Camera Powers On

NASA’s Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope has powered on its primary optical components one million miles from Earth. Featuring a field of view 100 times larger than Hubble, it will image thousands of galaxies in single panoramic exposures!
