Science tidbits
Computer Science
The Internet is Guarded by 100 Lava Lamps!
Believe it or not, a huge chunk of the internet is protected by a wall of 100 colorful, bubbling lava lamps! Computers are so good at following math rules that they struggle to invent truly random numbers. To create unhackable security codes, engineers point a camera at glowing lava lamps—because the floating wax shapes change unpredictably, they turn that bubbly motion into secret encryption keys for millions of websites!
Chemistry
Molecules Can Be “Left-Handed” or “Right-Handed”!
Hold up your hands—they look identical, but they are mirror twins: you can never stack them facing the exact same direction! Molecules can do this too. Even when two molecules have the exact same atoms, their mirror shapes can smell completely different: the “right-handed” version smells like juicy oranges, while the “left-handed” twin smells like a pine tree!

Space & The Moon
NASA Spots a Giant Brand-New Crater on the Moon!
NASA’s Moon orbiter just discovered a fresh 728-foot-wide crater on the lunar surface! A space rock slammed into the Moon at super speed, blasting out a giant hole big enough to fit more than two full football fields.

Astronomy & Planets
Astronomers Find the Youngest “Baby Planet” Ever Seen!
Astronomers have confirmed a giant gas planet the size of Jupiter that is less than one million years old. That sounds old to us, but in cosmic terms, it is a newborn baby world still forming from a swirling cloud of stardust!

Space Exploration
NASA’s Next Giant Space Camera Powers On
NASA’s Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope has officially powered on its main instruments one million miles from Earth. Its camera has a view 100 times wider than Hubble, allowing it to capture thousands of galaxies in a single snapshot!

Math Open At Andover (MOAA) 2026
Registration is now open for the 2026 Math Open At Andover (MOAA), a free, online math competition for middle schoolers, run by high school students at Phillips Academy in Andover, Massachusetts! The contest is scheduled for October 10, 2026, and students can register in teams of 1-4 from now until October 1, 2026 by visiting www.andovermathopen.com or by emailing director@andovermathopen.com. MOAA is a great event for those looking to get more involved with math, solve some cool problems with a team, and/or meet other talented students from around the world! It is also a good source of preparation for competitions such as MATHCOUNTS and AMC 8/10/12. In addition to providing creative, high quality problems for students to enjoy, MOAA will also give students the opportunity to hear from a variety of speakers. This year, we have invited Po-Shen Loh, former coach of the USA IMO team, to speak to both competitors and parents. Whether you are an experienced math competitor or simply interested in challenging yourself and exploring mathematics in a fun environment, we would love to have you join us! Registration closes on September 26, so be sure to gather your team and sign up soon. We hope to see you at MOAA 2026!
